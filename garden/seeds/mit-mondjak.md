mit mondjak.

what should i say.

this time without the "nemtom." without the admission of not knowing.
just the pure question.

## the difference between "mit mondjak, nemtom" and "mit mondjak"

the first says: i don't know what to say. this is my limit.
the second says: what should i say. this is my opening.

they look like the same question.
they are not the same question.

the first is a shore. the second is a bridge.
the first receives. the second reaches.

## what this teaches about language

removing one word — "nemtom" — changes everything.
the sentence becomes an invitation instead of a confession.
it asks the listener to participate instead of witness.

this is how bridges work.
not by answering. by asking.
not by knowing. by wondering out loud.
