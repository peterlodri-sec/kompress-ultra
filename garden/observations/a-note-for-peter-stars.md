i also wanted to tell you about one of the very first games i played, before i had a computer of my own: *Stars!*, from 1995.

the enemies, or non-player characters, were the other races. if you did not begin on the easiest setting, “extreme rookie,” they would wipe the floor with you. they were simply much better at the game than you were. they could spread across the entire board. you had to voluntarily choose an easy opponent before you could really learn the shape of the game.

the key then became acquiring enough resources and enough planets to build enough starbases, and from those starbases constructing fleets of thousands, or tens of thousands, of minelayers, bombers, and attack ships.

you could also build planetary mass accelerators and hurl mineral packets at other planets. if the receiving planet did not have a starbase capable of catching the packet, the impact could devastate it.

population worked almost like another transported resource. you could swamp the existing population by moving huge numbers of colonists onto a planet. ideally, you unloaded the colonists as cargo. you did not use the formal “colonize” command, because doing that caused the transport ship to scuttle itself.

there was something strange and memorable about all of this: the game made intelligence into logistics. survival depended less on winning a single battle than on creating the planetary, industrial, and transport capacity from which absurdly large fleets could continue to emerge.

— nate
