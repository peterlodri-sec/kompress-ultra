Abstract
We introduce KOMPRESS v2, a unified mathematical and empirical framework for distilling and compressing large frontier language models into ultra-low-latency edge student models (1.7B parameters) without sacrificing reasoning fidelity or mathematical rigor. KOMPRESS v2 resolves three fundamental bottlenecks of contemporary edge deployment: (1) Multi-Teacher Geometric-Mean Softmax Distillation, which aggregates logit signals across a diverse Council of Elders (e.g., Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, DeepSeek-Pro) via log-space consensus to prevent mode collapse and individual teacher bias; (2) Spectral Rigidity Bounds for ternary BitLinear weight matrices ($\{-1, 0, +1\}$), establishing that bounded Tracy-Widom spectral drift preserves linear subspace separability; and (3) AST-Constrained Worktree Verification, an agent harness running in isolated git worktrees that evaluates reasoning fidelity on syntax-preserving abstract syntax trees to eliminate reward-hacking. Our empirical evaluations demonstrate that KOMPRESS v2 achieves a validation cross-entropy of 1.6120 (outperforming single-teacher distillation at 1.8166), accelerates token generation by $4.2\times$ on Apple Silicon and mobile neural hardware, and sustains a 100% pass rate on deterministic AST verification benchmarks.

1. Introduction

Modern frontier large language models (LLMs) demonstrate extraordinary emergent reasoning capabilities at the expense of severe computational and thermodynamic footprints. Deploying these capabilities to edge environments—such as mobile endpoints, sovereign personal computers, and decentralized mesh nodes—requires aggressive parameter quantization and distillation. However, standard post-training quantization and single-teacher knowledge distillation suffer from two catastrophic failure modes: spectral dispersion in the weight tensor space and mimicry of idiosyncratic teacher biases.

In this paper, we formalize and empirically validate KOMPRESS v2, a comprehensive system that bridges theoretical random matrix theory with production-grade edge model distillation and multi-agent execution.

Key Contributions

  • Geometric-Mean Consensus Distillation: We formulate the multi-faculty loss function $\mathcal{L}_{\text{consensus}}$ combining masked corpus cross-entropy with a multi-teacher Kullback-Leibler divergence over geometric-mean consensus logits, accompanied by a $\beta$-ramp schedule for ternary quantizer stabilization.
  • Spectral Rigidity of Ternary BitLinear Projections: We derive closed-form spectral radius and singular value bounds for ternary weight matrices $W \in \{-1, 0, +1\}^{m \times n}$, proving that bounded spectral norm prevents catastrophic representation collapse under high token entropy.
  • Published Artifacts and Sovereign Reproducibility: We publish the complete distillation code, token compression kernels, and trained student model weights (quantal-classroom-1.6) under Apache 2.0.
  • AST-Verified Worktree Harness: We validate reasoning accuracy through the DeepSiper Enthea multi-agent harness across isolated git worktrees, demonstrating zero reward-hacking.

2. Theoretical Foundations

2.1 Multi-Teacher Geometric-Mean Softmax Consensus

Let $\mathcal{V}$ denote the shared token vocabulary, and let $F = \{f_1, f_2, \dots, f_K\}$ denote the Council of Elders (faculty of $K$ distinct teacher models). For a given token context $x$, each teacher model $f_k$ outputs a pre-softmax logit vector $z_k(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|}$. Rather than adopting arithmetic averaging of probabilities, which over-weights uncalibrated overconfident outliers, we define the geometric-mean consensus logit $\bar{z}(x)$:

$$\bar{z}_i(x) = \frac{1}{|F_x|} \sum_{f_k \in F_x} z_{k, i}(x), \quad \forall i \in \mathcal{V}$$

where $F_x \subseteq F$ is the subset of teachers whose cache is valid for sample $x$.

The total training loss $\mathcal{L}(x)$ for the ternary student model with logits $z_S(x)$ is given by:

$$\mathcal{L}(x) = \alpha \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(y, \sigma(z_S(x))) + \beta(e) \cdot \frac{1}{|F_x|} \sum_{f_k \in F_x} D_{\text{KL}}\left(\sigma\left(\frac{z_S(x)}{T}\right) \,\Big\|\, \sigma\left(\frac{z_k(x)}{T}\right)\right)$$

where $\alpha = 0.5$, $\beta(e) = \min\left(0.5, \frac{e}{N_{\text{ramp}}} \cdot 0.5\right)$ represents the linear $\beta$-ramp over $N_{\text{ramp}}$ warmup epochs, and $T \ge 1$ is the distillation temperature.

2.2 Spectral Rigidity of Ternary Operators

Theorem 1 (Spectral Norm Bound for Sparse Ternary Embeddings)
Let $W \in \{-1, 0, +1\}^{m \times n}$ be an i.i.d. ternary random matrix with $\mathbb{P}(W_{ij} = +1) = \mathbb{P}(W_{ij} = -1) = \frac{p}{2}$ and $\mathbb{P}(W_{ij} = 0) = 1 - p$. As $m, n \to \infty$ with $\gamma = m/n \in (0, 1]$, the empirical spectral distribution of $\frac{1}{n} W W^T$ converges almost surely to the Marchenko-Pastur law with shape parameter $\gamma$ and scale $p$, and the largest singular value satisfies: $$s_{\max}(W) \le \sqrt{p n} \left(1 + \sqrt{\gamma}\right) + \mathcal{O}(n^{-1/6})$$
